Lesson one · 3 minutes

Whiskey is the family. The rules depend on where it’s from.

Whiskey starts as grain, gets fermented and distilled, then spends time in wood. Grain recipe, still, cask, place, and local rules shape what ends up in the glass. The spelling is a clue—“whisky” is common in Scotland, “whiskey” in the U.S. and Ireland—but it is not a law of nature.

BourbonAmerican whiskey made from a mash that is at least 51% corn. Straight bourbon is matured in new, charred oak; expect possible vanilla, caramel, fruit, and oak.
RyeU.S. rye whiskey starts with at least 51% rye grain. Spice, pepper, mint, or herbs are common clues, but recipe and aging keep it from being a one-note style.
ScotchWhisky made and matured in Scotland for at least three years in oak casks. Malt, grain, region, cask, and peat-smoke choices span orchard fruit to brine and smoke.
Aging, proof, cask finish, and blending all change the result. “Older” and “more expensive” are not synonyms for “better.” Ask what the guest likes, then point to one good match.
